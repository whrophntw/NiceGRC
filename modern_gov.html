    Modern Governance Thought


    Why does "Governance" exist?

    Fundamentally, it provides top-down management of operational best practices for reducing business risk. Practically, it's mandated by B2B contracts and regulators. ISO27001, NIST 800-53, SOC2 type II are just a few of the Standards that must be adhered to in order to operate contractually/legally.

    These Standards require artifacts that become both technical and abstract. There are the abstract "policies and procedures" approved by Directors. Then there are agents that take autonomous action based on Policies (soul.md, config.yaml, semantic memory markdown files) and Procedures (every skill markdown file).

    The latter are obviously lower-level operational governance examples, but consider who is tasked with establishing agent configuration standards (Software Engineering Manager). Who does that individual report to (Director) and what is that person's risk tolerance and business objective? (Established by CISO)

    Software Engineers have gone from looking through 3 pull requests a week to 300 with modern coding agent workflows. To prevent insanity, governance, risk management, and compliance operations must be automated in such a way that it becomes a "product" that generates artifacts. Practically checking compliance boxes but fundamentally reducing risk to the business.
