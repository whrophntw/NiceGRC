When the diagram says "Memories," it's not referring to device RAM. It's referring to the agent's own stores of information, which come in three flavors:

  • Episodic memory: the system logs: a record of past sessions, retrievable and searchable after the fact. This is how the agent answers "what were we working on last Tuesday?" without you repeating yourself.

  • Semantic memory: durable facts and knowledge, saved deliberately across sessions. The kind of thing worth not re-learning: who you are, how your environment is wired, which conventions you insist on. I'll never forget the first time a casual session of researching a topic with ChatGPT and becoming spooked because it referred to information I gave it in a prompt a few weeks prior. This is Semantic Memory.

  • Procedural memory: the steps to complete a task. when agent reason through a complex task that results in failure, if it discovers the correct sequence of events it will write a file containing procedural instructions and key word triggers to repeat the steps without failure during future attempts. These skill files are an example of Procedural Memory.
