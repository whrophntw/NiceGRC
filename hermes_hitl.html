Human in the (Way) Loop


In ai testing, deployment, and workflows there is a principal called Human-in-the-Loop. It is meant to supervise or "sanity check" autonomous operations.

In practice, it can be seen as Human-in-the-Way (of progress). I am not vehemently for or against the principal either way. Like most things in life, moderation is best and often contextual. Yes, businesses need to remain competitive, but fundamental forethought or gut instinct should not be thrown out because of it.

There are two narratives currently circulating that support HITL unknowingly. The first, "Computer Science degrees are cooked because coding agents do all the junior work". The second, "Interviewers and Software Engineering Managers are asking people to explain what their code does and why they made certain design decisions, just to find out the Engineers don't know".

Just because you can automate something doesn't mean you always should. If for no other reason, when it is time to strategicaly pivot infrastructure or platform, you understand what the tradeoffs are. I've seen some complaints that platforms under Anthropic or Grok are increasing costs substantially. What did people think was going to happen?

Human In The Loop should be leveraged as old-fashioned human instinct.

"Gee, I don't know team. There's about 30 LLMs across 4 trusted providers. Should we really build our entire department's operations around one model that also develops the tool we depend on?"

Even if you "automate the hard way" by building custom python scripts and workflows that empower weaker models. It pays off in the long run by your team actually understanding what's going on under the hood. You get to operate with substantially cheaper models. You're still exercising your brain to think critically about development decisions. You still end up exponentially increasing efficiency in the long term without a substantial amount of technical debt.
